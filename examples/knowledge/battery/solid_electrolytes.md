# Solid electrolytes for lithium batteries (reference notes)

## Sulfide electrolytes

Argyrodite sulfide electrolytes (e.g. Li6PS5Cl) exhibit room-temperature ionic
conductivities on the order of 1-10 mS/cm, comparable to liquid electrolytes.
Their softness allows dense interfaces to form under modest pressure. However,
sulfides are highly sensitive to moisture and evolve toxic H2S on exposure to
air, which complicates dry-room manufacturing and raises cost.

## Oxide electrolytes

Garnet oxides such as LLZO (Li7La3Zr2O12) are chemically stable against lithium
metal and non-flammable, but their room-temperature conductivity is typically
lower than sulfides and they require high-temperature sintering (>1000 C), which
is energy intensive and hard to scale cheaply.

## Interfaces and dendrites

The dominant failure mode in solid-state cells is lithium dendrite propagation
along grain boundaries and voids in the electrolyte, especially at high charging
rates (above ~1-4C). Thin protective interlayers and interface coatings have
been shown to raise critical current density, but long-term durability over
1000 cycles under fast charging remains unproven at scale.

## Interface resistance

Reported interface resistances between lithium metal and solid electrolytes
often exceed 100 ohm-cm^2 without engineering, and dropping below ~30 ohm-cm^2
at room temperature typically requires interlayers or applied stack pressure.
