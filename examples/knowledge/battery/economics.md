# Solid-state battery economics (reference notes)

## Cost targets

Automotive lithium-ion pack costs have historically been benchmarked around
$100-130/kWh, with a widely cited $80/kWh threshold for cost parity with
internal-combustion vehicles. Solid-state cells add cost from the solid
electrolyte itself, from dry-room or inert-atmosphere processing, and from
lower initial manufacturing yields.

## Manufacturing

Sulfide-based cells may reuse roll-to-roll processing similar to conventional
lithium-ion, which is favorable for scale-up. Oxide-based cells generally
require sintering steps that do not map onto existing gigafactory tooling,
raising capital cost. Yield at scale is the dominant unknown in every published
cost model; a process that works in the lab can be uneconomical if defect rates
stay high.

## Energy density

Energy densities above 500 Wh/kg at the cell level generally assume a lithium
metal anode paired with a high-voltage cathode. The solid electrolyte's mass and
the stack pressure hardware both cut into system-level (pack) energy density,
so cell-level figures overstate what a vehicle actually gets.
